Part two · how it actually works

Presentations are about change.

Companies start up, grow, mature and eventually decline. To avoid the decline, an organisation has to keep shifting. And to shift, somebody has to persuade the people in it.

If you have no change to propose, you do not need a presentation. You need an email.

Starting off on the right foot
  1. Great communicators are mostly made. The part you can train is the part that decides the room
  2. An effective presentation is prepared spontaneity: strategy, self-awareness, and practice
  3. Communication = content + relationship with the audience
  4. You communicate with words — but also numbers, charts, images, your body, your voice
  5. Slides are a support. They are not the presentation
I never could make a good impromptu speech without several hours to prepare it.Mark Twain, 1879

Tip 1 · connect with the audience

Aristotle said persuasion needs three things.

Ethos · ethical appeal

What it is

Shared values and experiences, to gain credibility.

Why it matters

The audience feels connected, and has respect for your idea.

Sounds like

“I ran this desk for six years, and I got this wrong twice.”

Without it

They liked the idea and did not trust the person holding it.

Pathos · emotional appeal

What it is

Feelings of pleasure and pain, to stir imagination.

Why it matters

People make important decisions based on emotions like these.

Sounds like

Not “churn rose to nine per cent”, but “the client who left on Friday had been with us eleven years.”

Without it

They followed every slide, and did nothing on Monday.

Logos · logical appeal

What it is

Structure, words and data to support your idea. Evidence.

Why it matters

It is necessary in all presentations, to convince your audience.

Sounds like

One number, one comparison, one source. Then the claim they hold up.

Without it

They believed you in the room, and could not defend it to their boss.

All three are necessary. None of them is sufficient on its own.

What each part of that pitch was doing

Ethos

The move

The hard part

$450,000 already sold. A track record is the one kind of credibility nobody argues with.

Pathos

The move

One pair donated for every pair sold.

The hard part

It stops being a sock and becomes a reason. Nobody in that room had to be talked into caring.

Logos

The move

The ask, the equity, the sales, and what the product does that another sock does not.

The hard part

It is what lets an investor defend the deal to somebody who felt nothing.

In studies of these pitches, pathos is the most used appeal of the three, more than half of everything said. Logos comes second. Ethos is used least of all.

Four sharks passed. One invested. Same pitch, same three appeals, and the room weighed them differently — different experience, different priorities.

Know your audience and you can lean on one of the three. If you do not know them, go for the balance.

Thirty seconds · one possible briefing

Solis began the quarter as a 3% position, safely inside our limit. Its rally has pushed it to 9% without us buying a single share. We now have to choose between cutting our highest-conviction investment and accepting almost twice our permitted concentration. I recommend reducing it to 5%, because portfolio discipline should not depend on how much we like the stock.

Northwind paid us in 42 days a year ago, safely inside our policy. It now takes 51, and the credit committee never approved it. We have to choose between tightening the terms on our largest renewal and accepting that sales can lend our cash without asking. I recommend enforcing the 45 days, because credit policy should not depend on who the customer is.

Tip 3 · use contrast

Put two realities in tension.

You have just turned a fact into a story. This is the engine that was inside it.

Last year we hired forty analysts.

True, precise, and nobody in the room does anything with it.

We hired forty. Thirty-eight left.

We are not building a team. We are running a training programme for our competitors.

Neither number is new to that company. Both were already in the system. Side by side, they are a decision.

Four ways to put two realities in tension  ·  Intended / emerged  ·  Expected / actual  ·  Benefit / cost  ·  Success / risk  ·  you will pick one in a moment

Tip 3 · why the gap works

Use contrast to make it appealing.

Neither number was new. So why did the pair land?

People are naturally attracted to opposites, so presentations should draw from this attraction to create interest. Communicating an idea juxtaposed with its polar opposite creates energy. Moving back and forth between the contradictory poles encourages engagement from the audience.

Interest

A gap is an open question. You cannot help wanting it closed, and that is what buys your next two minutes.

Energy

Alone, a fact is a description. Against its opposite it has something at stake, and a shape they can carry out.

Engagement

Not one jump. Back and forth between the poles. Which is what you are about to do.

Tip 3b · balance analytical and emotional appeal

How much emotion can you use?

Where it helps them understand what is at stake — usually the opening and the close. Never by touching the evidence.

In this room logos is free. It is your native language, and the analysis is also where your credibility comes from. Pathos is the one nobody else is teaching you.

There is no fixed ratio, so this is a judgement and not a formula. Modify the presentation to map the needs of your audience.

Get it wrong and you damage your credibility in both directions.

Tip 5 · presentation in action

The words carry the claim. Everything else decides whether they believe it.

Verbal

The actual words. They carry the argument, and they are the part a model can write for you.

Meta-verbal

Volume, tone, pace, pauses. The use of the voice.

Non-verbal

Face, gestures, posture. What the room reads while you are still talking.

Same nine words

“Is this the proposal you want me to support?”

“Is this the proposal that you want me to support?”

And the market is already paying for it

Models now score the vocal delivery of earnings calls. How it was said carries information the transcript does not, the market reacts in real time, and delivery degrades most on bad news.

Baik, Kim, Kim & Yoon, «Vocal delivery quality in earnings conference calls», Journal of Accounting and Economics 80(1), 2025

The words are the part a model can write for you. How you say them is the part that is still yours.

Tip 5 · what to actually do

Ten decisions, and you make all of them before you start talking.

The voice
  • Volume set to the room, not to your nerves
  • Rate around 130 to 150 words a minute — three minutes is roughly 400 words, with room to pause
  • Pitch decides which word carries the meaning
  • Unfilled pauses work. “Um” does not
The body
  • Move with intention, not with nerves
  • Eye contact: rest your gaze on one person at a time
  • No fidgeting, no locked feet
